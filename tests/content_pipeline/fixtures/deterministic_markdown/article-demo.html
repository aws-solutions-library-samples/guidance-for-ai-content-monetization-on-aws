Electric vehicle adoption accelerates across European fleets

Fleet operators are electrifying faster than private buyers, reshaping charging demand and residual values across Europe.

By Lena Hartmann · August 3, 2026 · Automotive

Advertisement Leaderboard placeholder — 728x90

Corporate fleets have quietly become the engine of Europe's electric vehicle transition. New registrations data for the first half of 2026 show battery-electric models taking more than a third of fleet purchases in Germany, the Netherlands, and the Nordics, well ahead of the private market. Total-cost-of-ownership math, not sentiment, is doing the work: lower energy and maintenance costs now offset higher list prices within three years for high-mileage vehicles.

Charging infrastructure is following the money. Depot charging installations grew faster than public fast charging for the fourth consecutive quarter, as logistics operators lock in overnight electricity rates. Grid connection lead times remain the most cited obstacle, with operators in several markets reporting waits of a year or more for high-capacity depot upgrades.

The used market is the next test. As three-year leases written during the 2023 fleet boom expire, a wave of used electric vehicles is reaching dealers. Residual values have stabilized after 2024's sharp correction, helped by battery health certificates becoming standard at resale. Analysts caution that the balance remains fragile: a price war among manufacturers could undercut used values again and slow the replacement cycle that fleets depend on.

Policy still matters at the margins. Purchase incentives have largely been wound down, but favorable benefit-in-kind taxation continues to steer company car drivers toward electric options in most large European markets, and city low-emission zones keep tightening the case for diesel replacement.
