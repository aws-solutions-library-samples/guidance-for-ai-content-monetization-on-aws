# Continuous Glucose Monitors Reshape Type 2 Diabetes Screening

Continuous glucose monitors (CGMs), once reserved for insulin-dependent patients, are moving into routine type 2 diabetes screening after a 2026 clinical consensus statement from the American Diabetes Association endorsed their use for at-risk adults.

## What changed

Traditional screening relies on a fasting blood draw or an HbA1c test that reflects a three-month average. Both miss post-meal glucose spikes that are among the earliest signs of insulin resistance. A CGM worn for 14 days captures roughly 1,300 readings, letting clinicians see the daily pattern rather than a single point.

In a 4,000-patient study across 12 health systems, two weeks of CGM data reclassified 19% of patients who had "normal" HbA1c results into a pre-diabetic range, prompting earlier lifestyle intervention.

## Cost and access

Sensor prices have fallen to about $35 per 14-day unit at scale, and three major insurers added CGM screening to preventive coverage in 2026. Endocrinologists caution that data interpretation still requires clinician training, and that over-monitoring can cause anxiety in otherwise healthy patients.

## What comes next

Device makers are piloting algorithms that flag glucose-variability patterns automatically, reducing the interpretation burden. Regulators are expected to rule on the first over-the-counter screening CGM in late 2026.
