Electric Vehicle Adoption Accelerates as Battery Costs Fall Below $80/kWh

Battery pack prices fell below the symbolic $80 per kilowatt-hour mark in the first quarter of 2026, according to a BloombergNEF survey of 210 automakers and cell suppliers. Analysts have long treated $100/kWh as the point at which an electric vehicle costs the same to build as a comparable gasoline car; the new figure means many mid-size EVs now reach sticker-price parity without subsidies.

The decline is driven by lithium iron phosphate (LFP) chemistry, which uses no cobalt or nickel, and by manufacturing scale in North America and Europe. Ford, Hyundai, and Volkswagen each confirmed model lines that will switch to LFP packs during the 2026 model year.

Charging infrastructure remains the largest adoption barrier. The survey found that buyers in regions with fewer than 15 fast chargers per 100,000 residents were three times more likely to abandon an EV purchase. Federal and state programs disbursed $7.5 billion for charging build-out through 2025, but installation has lagged funding by roughly 18 months.

Used-EV pricing has stabilized after a volatile 2024, giving fleet operators more confidence in residual values. Industry groups now project electric models will make up 32% of new light-vehicle sales in the United States by 2028.
